# MagicBox GTM Plan

**Product:** MagicBox (`magicboxai.in`)  
**Decision:** launch and test the full MagicBox ambition  
**Markets:** India, US, UK  
**Updated:** 2026-07-25

## 1. GTM thesis

MagicBox should be sold as an AI marketing operating system for small teams that need to create,
review, schedule, publish, and learn from content without hiring a full content team.

Do not force the market into one narrow product story too early. Lead with the full ambition:

- social automation from one weekly brief;
- brand-aware captions, images, and post plans;
- UGC-style/video creative workflows where the feature is available;
- approval and scheduling;
- connected publishing for supported channels;
- a guided setup that learns what each customer actually needs.

The first goal is not only revenue. The first goal is to discover which customer group has the
strongest urgent pull, which feature creates the "I need this now" moment, and what customers will
pay for repeatedly.

## 2. Target customers

Run the first GTM pass across these customer groups:

| Segment | Who | Core pain | MagicBox angle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solo founders | SaaS, AI tools, devtools, small B2B products | They know they should post but do not have a system | Weekly founder update to LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube, and short-form assets |
| SaaS founders | Early-stage B2B SaaS and AI startups | Product updates do not become distribution | Turn launches, changelogs, demos, and founder POV into approved content |
| D2C brands | Food, beauty, wellness, fashion, local ecommerce | They need consistent visual/social content | Brand-aware campaigns, reels, product posts, seasonal drops |
| Consultants and agencies | Marketing, design, growth, software, automation | Client work creates repeating content demand | Draft first-pass client content, proposals, and social calendars faster |
| Service businesses | Clinics, studios, local B2B services, training, IT services | They rely on trust but publish inconsistently | Convert expertise and customer stories into recurring posts |

## 3. Geographic focus

Start with India, US, and UK.

Use different entry angles by market:

- **India:** founder-led SaaS, AI startups, D2C brands, consultants, service businesses that are active on LinkedIn and Instagram.
- **US:** SaaS founders, creator-led D2C brands, agencies, consultants, operators already using AI tools.
- **UK:** consultants, service businesses, small SaaS teams, and founder-led B2B companies with regular LinkedIn activity.

## 4. Positioning

Primary promise:

> Turn your product, brand, or weekly business update into ready-to-review social content and marketing assets.

Sharper ICP-specific hooks:

- **Solo founders:** "Stop staring at a blank LinkedIn box. Turn one weekly update into a real content plan."
- **SaaS founders:** "Every feature launch should become distribution. MagicBox turns product updates into posts, visuals, and clips."
- **D2C brands:** "Create product and brand content faster without losing your voice."
- **Consultants/agencies:** "Produce first drafts and campaign ideas faster, then review before anything goes live."
- **Service businesses:** "Turn expertise, client questions, and wins into consistent social proof."

## 5. Offer ladder

Start with a guided customer-input motion:

1. **Free product audit call:** review their current social/content workflow.
2. **MagicBox setup session:** build their brand profile and generate first assets.
3. **7-day content sprint:** produce a first weekly plan plus at least one video/visual asset if applicable.
4. **Paid beta / Pro conversion:** convert users who see value into Pro, Max, or a higher-touch plan.

Do not over-commit to custom service delivery unless capacity exists. It is fine to offer hands-on onboarding while keeping publishing approvals under the customer's control.

## 6. Lead sourcing

Build lead lists in small batches, then qualify manually.

Search criteria:

- title: founder, co-founder, CEO, owner, marketing lead, head of growth, consultant, agency owner;
- company size: 1-50 first, then 51-200 for D2C/agencies;
- countries: India, United States, United Kingdom;
- signals: active LinkedIn/Instagram/YouTube/X presence, recent product launches, D2C drops, founder posts, agency content, "we help" service positioning;
- exclude: large companies with full marketing teams unless they show a single-owner workflow.

Data to collect:

- name, title, company, country;
- website and public social links;
- LinkedIn profile;
- business email if available and compliant;
- why they fit MagicBox;
- suggested outreach angle;
- status: research, contacted, replied, call booked, beta, paid, not fit.

## 7. Outreach motion

Keep the first pass founder-led and personal.

Daily operating cadence:

- 20 researched leads per market per week;
- 10 high-quality personalized follow-ups per day maximum;
- 5-10 thoughtful comments on ICP posts per day;
- 3 founder posts per week showing real MagicBox workflows;
- 2 short demos per week using safe demo brands or permissioned customer examples.

Do not mass-DM or automate unsolicited outreach. The goal is learning and trust, not volume.

## 8. Outreach angles

Use a different first line per segment:

- SaaS founder: reference a launch, feature, changelog, demo, or founder post.
- D2C brand: reference a product drop, Instagram cadence, or visual campaign gap.
- Consultant/agency: reference repeated client content workload.
- Service business: reference trust-building and educational posts.

CTA options:

- "Want me to turn one of your recent updates into a MagicBox content plan?"
- "Can I make a 3-post sample from your website and latest announcement?"
- "Would you try a 7-day MagicBox sprint and tell me what breaks?"

## 9. Customer input system

For every call, capture:

- What do they currently use to create posts/videos?
- Who approves content?
- Which channel matters most?
- How often do they post now?
- What content gets delayed or skipped?
- What would they pay for: planning, captions, images, UGC/video, scheduling, publishing, analytics, or agency workflow?
- What feels risky: AI quality, brand voice, approvals, platform permissions, pricing, compliance?
- What output made them react strongest?

After each demo, score:

- urgency: 1-5;
- willingness to pay: 1-5;
- trust in generated output: 1-5;
- setup friction: 1-5;
- best feature: social plan, captions, images, video, scheduling, publishing, brand kit, approvals.

## 10. Two-week execution plan

### Days 1-2: Prepare the selling surface

- Make `magicboxai.in` clearly support the full ambition without unsupported fake claims.
- Keep pricing visible but invite guided beta/customer-input calls.
- Create one demo workflow for SaaS, one for D2C, and one for consultant/service businesses.
- Add UTMs for India, US, and UK campaigns.

### Days 3-5: Build lead batches

- 30 India leads: SaaS founders, D2C owners, agencies, service businesses.
- 30 US leads: SaaS founders, creator-led brands, agencies, consultants.
- 30 UK leads: consultants, small SaaS, service businesses.
- Enrich only high-fit leads with business emails or verified social profiles.

### Days 6-10: Founder-led outreach

- Contact the top 10 per day with a specific sample offer.
- Publish build-in-public posts showing the workflow.
- Book 10-15 calls.
- Convert the best 5 into guided beta users.

### Days 11-14: Learn and decide

- Review which segment booked calls fastest.
- Review which feature made prospects most interested.
- Update landing sections and demos based on real objections.
- Decide what gets more engineering focus for the next sprint.

## 11. Success metrics

By the end of the first two weeks:

- 90 researched leads across India, US, UK;
- 50 personalized outbound touches;
- 10-15 customer calls;
- 5 guided beta users;
- 3 permissioned workflow examples or anonymized before/after examples;
- at least 1 paying customer or strong written buying intent;
- a ranked list of which segment and feature to double down on.

## 12. Open questions

1. Who will personally handle the first 50 outbound messages?
2. Should guided beta be free for feedback, paid from day one, or free with a conversion deadline?
3. What is the minimum support promise for the first beta users?
4. Are we comfortable showing UGC/video workflows on the public site immediately, or should they be demo-led until the operational path is proven?
5. Which CRM should track the first leads: a local CSV, Airtable, Google Sheet, or the MagicBox admin/backend?
